Banku

Banku is a cherished staple food in Ghana, particularly among the Ewe and Ga-Dangme people. It is made by fermenting corn and cassava dough and cooking them together into a smooth, slightly sour dough-like consistency. Traditionally eaten with grilled tilapia, pepper sauce, or okra stew, Banku is valued not only for its unique flavor but also for its role in bringing families and communities together at meal times. Its fermentation process reflects the deep-rooted traditions of Ghanaian food culture, where patience and natural methods are central to creating rich, nourishing dishes. Today, Banku continues to be a symbol of Ghanaian hospitality and heritage, often served during celebrations and gatherings.

Cooking Instructions

Click to view instructions
  1. Mix the dough: Combine equal parts of fermented corn dough and cassava dough in a bowl. Stir until smooth and free of lumps.
  2. Add water: Gradually add water to the mixture to achieve a thick, pourable consistency.
  3. Start cooking: Pour the mixture into a pot and place over medium heat. Stir continuously with a wooden spatula to prevent lumps.
  4. Cook and stir: As it heats, the mixture will thicken. Keep stirring vigorously to ensure even cooking and smooth texture.
  5. Adjust consistency: Add small amounts of water if the dough becomes too thick and difficult to stir.
  6. Final thickening: Cook for about 10–15 minutes until the Banku pulls away slightly from the sides of the pot and is smooth, stretchy, and glossy.
  7. Shape and serve: Wet your hands with water, scoop portions of the Banku, and roll into balls. Serve immediately with soup, stew, or grilled fish.

Ingredients:

  • 2 cups fermented corn dough
  • 2 cups cassava dough
  • 2–3 cups water (adjust as needed for consistency)
  • Salt to taste
  • Optional: cooked chicken, beef, or fish for serving

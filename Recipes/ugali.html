Ugali

Ugali is a staple food widely consumed in East Africa, especially in countries like Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda. Made from maize flour (cornmeal) and water, it is cooked into a dense, firm porridge that serves as a versatile accompaniment to stews, vegetables, and grilled meats. Ugali is more than just food—it is a symbol of sustenance and communal eating. Families often gather around a single plate, using their hands to scoop portions and share a meal together, reflecting the region’s values of community, hospitality, and tradition. Its simplicity and adaptability have made Ugali a daily dietary cornerstone, and it continues to be an essential element in both everyday meals and festive occasions across East Africa.

Cooking Instructions

Click to view instructions
  1. Boil water: Pour 2–3 cups of water into a pot and bring it to a rolling boil.
  2. Add maize flour: Gradually add 1–2 cups of maize (corn) flour to the boiling water while stirring continuously with a wooden spoon or spatula.
  3. Stir continuously: Keep stirring to prevent lumps and ensure the mixture thickens evenly.
  4. Cook to firmness: As the mixture thickens, reduce heat to low and continue stirring. Ugali should form a dense, firm dough that pulls away from the sides of the pot.
  5. Shape and serve: Wet a spoon or your hands with water, scoop portions of Ugali, and shape into balls or mounds. Serve immediately with stews, vegetables, or grilled meats.

Ingredients:

  • 2 cups maize (corn) flour
  • 2–3 cups water (adjust as needed for consistency)
  • Salt to taste
  • Optional: cooked chicken, beef, or fish for serving

Tagine

Tagine is a celebrated North African dish, especially in Morocco, named after the traditional conical clay pot in which it is cooked. It typically combines meats, vegetables, and dried fruits slow-cooked with aromatic spices such as cinnamon, cumin, saffron, and ginger, resulting in a rich, flavorful stew. Tagine is valued not only for its complex taste but also for its cultural significance as a communal dish, bringing families and friends together around the table. The slow-cooking method in the tagine pot reflects the North African emphasis on patience and layering of flavors, where ingredients gently meld to create depth and harmony. Today, Tagine continues to be an iconic representation of Moroccan culinary heritage, often featured in festive occasions and shared meals.

Cooking Instructions

Click to view instructions
  1. Chop vegetables and cut meat into pieces.
  2. Marinate meat with olive oil, cumin, cinnamon, paprika, ginger, salt, and pepper.
  3. Sear meat in a tagine or heavy pot until browned.
  4. Layer onions, meat, vegetables, and dried fruits in the pot.
  5. Add a little broth or water, cover, and simmer for 1.5–2 hours.
  6. Stir in fresh herbs and optional lemon juice before serving.
  7. Serve hot with couscous, rice, or flatbread.

Ingredients:

  • 500g chicken, lamb, or beef, cut into pieces
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 2–3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 carrots, sliced
  • 1 zucchini, sliced
  • 1 bell pepper, chopped
  • 100g dried fruits (apricots, prunes, or raisins)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp cinnamon
  • 1 tsp ground ginger
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • 200 ml chicken or vegetable broth
  • Fresh parsley or cilantro for garnish
  • Optional: lemon juice
